# Home CCTV Hub — Visual Design Review (Sentinel theme, shipped build)

**Reviewer:** Agent 2 — independent visual design critic
**Date:** 2026-11-04 (Asia/Kolkata)
**Target:** the running app at `http://localhost:3000` (Next.js 16 / React 19 / Tailwind v4), dark "Sentinel" operator theme
**Verdict:** the shipped UI is a coherent, high-contrast operator console with a genuinely good shell and login, but the design system's own contract is only partly met: time notation is inconsistent (12 h vs 24 h), priority red still collides with failure red, micro-type goes to 9–10 px, the 4 px rhythm is not held, and two settings screens break horizontally on tablet/mobile. **Score 6.5/10.**

---

## 1. Scope & method

### 1.1 What was judged

The **shipped, running** UI — `src/app/globals.css` (`@theme` + `@layer components`), the React components in `src/components/**`, and the rendered pages at four viewports. `design/DESIGN-SYSTEM.md` was used **only as the reference** the implementation claims to satisfy; it is treated as a claim to be checked, not as the design. The redesign proposal (`design/REDESIGN-PROPOSAL.md`) was deliberately ignored.

Routes covered (17 + login): `/`, `/playback`, `/events`, `/events/<id>`, `/events/stats`, `/faces`, `/notifications`, `/cameras`, `/device`, `/settings`, `/settings/users`, `/settings/notifications`, `/settings/detection`, `/settings/audit-log`, `/settings/system`, `/account`, `/login`.

### 1.2 Audit-only compliance

Read-only. I did **not** modify `src/`, CSS, config, APIs, DB, tests, `.env` or CCTV behaviour; did not install packages; did not start/restart servers; did not perform mutating UI actions (no saves, deletes, test notifications, user creation). The only write was this file. Authentication used the documented path — `POST /api/auth/login` with the `.env` credentials into a Playwright `storageState` — exactly as instructed, against `localhost` only. Helper scripts ran from `/tmp` (`/tmp/hhc-measure.mjs`, `/tmp/hhc-probe*.mjs`); all browser work was GET navigation + DOM read.

### 1.3 Evidence

**Lead-captured (cited throughout):**
- 68 viewport screenshots: `audit/screenshots/{desktop,laptop,tablet,mobile}/<route>.png` at 1920×1080, 1440×900, 834×1112, 390×844.
- 68 full-page captures: `audit/screenshots/full-page/<viewport>/*-full.png`.
- 7 interaction states: `audit/screenshots/states/` (focus mode desktop/mobile, More sheet, events list/filters/inspector, object-detection mobile).
- `audit/screenshots/capture-metrics.json` — 68 entries, per-route status/h1 count/font histogram/target sizes/overflow/console errors.

The floating circular "N" bottom-left in dev captures is the Next.js dev indicator and was excluded from judgement.

**My independent measurements (same live app, separate Playwright session):**
- 64 route×viewport DOM measurement passes (computed styles, effective-background compositing, font histogram, target boxes, overflow/clipping, heading inventory, line counts via `Range.getClientRects`).
- Targeted probes: page-frame offsets per route; playback stage vs picture; sticky save-bar overlap rectangles; `.table--stack` computed layout at 390/834/1920; camera-tile overlay inventory; breakpoint walks at 390/430/720/721/768/834/1024/1025; 12 h vs 24 h census; control hit areas.
- **Contrast ratios computed numerically** from the shipped token values (WCAG 2.1 relative-luminance formula, alpha composited over the actual ancestor backgrounds) and cross-checked against rendered computed styles; **no ratio in this report is estimated**.
- Pixel measurement with Pillow on five screenshots to quantify letterboxing (playback, focus mode).
- Static census of `globals.css` (spacing declarations, font-size declarations, raw hex/rgba) and of component class usage (dead classes, arbitrary type sizes, hard-coded colours).

### 1.4 How to read severities

- **High** — wrong information, a visibly broken layout, or a design-system rule the product itself calls non-negotiable, on a primary screen/viewport.
- **Medium** — degrades scanning, hierarchy or responsive behaviour materially, but the screen still works.
- **Low** — polish/drift; fix in a consolidation pass.

## 3. Dimension analysis

### 3.1 Typography

**Scale in use (measured, rendered).** H1 = **24 px / 650 / −0.48 px** on 15 of 16 authenticated routes; mobile H1 = 20 px. The exception is **Playback**, whose H1 is **16 px / 600** at *every* viewport (`PageIntro compact` → `text-base`, `src/components/ui/States.tsx:88-96`, used at `PlaybackView.tsx:255`) — it is visually smaller than the 16 px camera/inspector card titles and reads as a card heading, not a page title (VIS-21).

**H2 is two different roles.** Card titles are `<h2>` at **13 px / 650** (dashboard "Parking" 13/600, settings "Environment", "Rules", "Keep events"), while the inspector title and camera name are **16 px / 650** (`globals.css:496,534`). The spec's H2 is 16 px, so most `<h2>`s ship one step small.

**Body is effectively 12 px.** `body` is declared 14 px (`globals.css:117`), but the rendered histogram over all 68 captures is dominated by 12 px: **45 749 of 64 464 nodes (71 %) at 12 px**, 6 036 at 13 px, only **299 at 14 px**. Reading text (`page-head__sub` 13 px, `.section__hint` 12 px, `.setting-row__desc` 12 px, `.rule__detail` 12 px) sits at 12–13 px — acceptable in a dense console, but it is **not** the specified Body role.

**Micro-type is the weakest link.** 9 px / 10 px survive in the shipped CSS (VIS-04), and the lead's capture counts **391 nodes at 9 px and 2 969 at 10 px**. The worst offenders are labels that are *structural*, not decorative: `.kv dt` 9 px (Device), `.cam-card__prop dt` 9 px (Camera setup), `.settings-nav__label` 9 px, `.table--stack td::before` 9 px (mobile tables), `.stat__label` / `.page-head__eyebrow` / `.nav__label` 10 px, `.face-card__time` 10 px. With line-heights of 13.5–15 px and uppercase + 0.1–0.16 em tracking, these are the hardest strings to read on the screen — and they carry meaning (prop names, nav groups, stat labels).

**Mono vs sans discipline: good, with two leaks.** Mono is correctly used for machine data: clock (`13 px`, tabular), event times (`12 px`), camera meta channels (`SUB 102`), IPs and IDs (`badge--mono`), table `.num`, timeline labels. Two minor leaks: mono carries **human prose** in `.camera__meta` ("Sub 102 · 111 events today", 10 px mono) and `.by-camera__count`; and some sans numeric text (`.stat__delta`, durations in `.event-row__meta`) has no `tabular-nums`, which is harmless because those values do not animate. "Tabular numerals" is declared on clock/stat/table/rail counts (spec-compliant), but is **not** declared on `.badge--mono` and `.face-card__time` (`font-variant-numeric: normal` measured) — however these inherit a monospace face, so digit widths are equal anyway; no visual jitter observed. So: the *rule* is stated and mostly followed, with the notation inconsistency of VIS-02 being the real typography defect.

**12 h vs 24 h (VIS-02)** is the typography/hierarchy problem with the highest operational cost: the operator reads `17:46:05` in the inspector and `05:46:54 pm` in the chrome, and `12:00 AM` in the filter that produced the list.

**Line length.** Mostly controlled: `page-head__sub` is capped at 70 ch (measured 573 px at 13 px), `.empty__text` 46 ch, `.setting-row__desc` 62 ch (469 px measured), login lede 44 ch. Two uncapped exceptions: `.section__hint` renders a **780 px single line (≈92 glyphs)** on Events statistics, and the read-only notice spans **1 420 px** on Settings. Fine as captions, but a long explanatory hint should share the 62–70 ch cap.

### 3.2 Spacing & alignment

**Rhythm.** 58 % of spacing declarations are off the 4 px grid (VIS-16). The visible result is small but cumulative: card headers 13 px tall padding vs card bodies 16 px, footers 11 px, rail rows 9 px, table cells 11/14, stat cards 12/14. Card interiors therefore feel slightly hand-placed rather than systematic.

**Density at 1920.** The components are dense at token level (rows 68 px, stat 82 px) but the *layouts* are not: 77 % of each event row and 65 % of each stat card are empty (VIS-08), notification rows are stretched to 1 640 px with a 1 110 px gap before the status pill, and the dashboard right rail ends ~300 px above the fold leaving a void under "Today by camera". At 390 px the opposite failure appears: the mobile page reserve is 204 px too generous (VIS-24), and the Object-detection page reaches **4 124 px**.

**Optical alignment.** App bar and content share an edge at ≥1025 px (both at 256 px measured). Below that they diverge: at 834 the brand starts at **16 px** while page content starts at **24 px (8 px off)**; at 390 it is **16 vs 12 px (4 px off)** (VIS-26). Card internals are otherwise well aligned: badge baselines, right-aligned numeric columns and the `.kv` grid all line up in the captures.

**Vertical rhythm.** Page section spacing (22 px `.section`, 18 px page-head margin, 14 px panel margins) is a 2 px ladder, consistent enough to read as intentional. The biggest vertical-space defects are the sticky bar overlap (VIS-07) and bottom reserve (VIS-24).

### 3.3 Colour

**Contrast: this is the strongest part of the shipped theme.** Every token pair used for text passes WCAG AA with margin, and the spec's numbers are honest (spec vs my computation): `--ink` 15.8 vs **15.82:1**, `--ink-2` 11.5 vs **11.50:1**, `--muted` 6.3 vs **6.34:1**, `--faint` 4.6 vs **5.44:1** (better than claimed), accent-ink on accent 10.4 vs **10.43:1**. Status text on its own tint (composited over card) all pass: online **8.13:1**, warn **8.51:1**, danger **6.07:1** (5.43:1 as rendered on a selected row), critical **6.39:1**, info **6.57:1**. Notice text is the best-contrast text in the app: warn **10.40:1**, danger **9.71:1**, accent **11.91:1** — but those three colours are hard-coded hexes, not tokens (VIS-18). Against **AAA** (7:1 for normal-size text) the primary and status inks pass (`ink` 15.82, `ink-2` 11.50, accent 11.18, online 10.28, warn 11.16, info 8.02, critical 8.01, danger 7.19 on card); only the metadata inks fall short of AAA (`muted` 6.34, `faint` 5.44) — expected for labels, and they still pass AA. The only sub-AA text anywhere is the decorative `.sep` separator glyph (VIS-25, 1.21–1.48:1). Disabled states drop below AA (`opacity .35` calendar dates 2.95:1, `opacity .45` buttons 3.27:1, `.rule[data-enabled=false]` muted text 3.20:1) — acceptable under WCAG's inactive-component exception, but `opacity` on whole subtrees is a blunt instrument and makes low-vision users' disabled states indistinguishable.

**Non-text boundaries are the weakness** (VIS-05): 1.24:1 hairlines, 1.53:1 control borders, 1.48:1 switch track — the design reads as "flat black-on-black with faint rules" rather than a layered console. The surface steps exist (`bg #070A0E` → `surface #0B1017` → `card #0F1620` → `card-2 #121B26` → `raised #16202D`) but adjacent steps are only 1.09–1.14:1 apart, so depth comes almost entirely from the hairlines and the accent — which is why the accent is doing so many jobs (VIS-19).

**Semantic collisions (this is the design-system's own red line).** Spec §2.3: "red never means 'person' or 'alarm recording' again… HIGH = warn amber". Shipped: red = HIGH priority (19 badges in one viewport), failure (`Failed`), offline dot, and destructive action (Delete/Sign out everywhere) (VIS-03). Teal = action + active nav + selection + "New" + role badges + normal-event dots + Log out (VIS-19); health chips correctly use green. Green `--online` is used correctly for online/live *and* for recording segments. Amber appears as warnings, as HIGH-priority badges in the rule editor, and as HIGH-priority event markers on the timeline (spec-consistent), while the timeline paints **alarm recordings red** (`bg-offline/85`) and **normal events teal** — so hue is doing classification work that the spec says must be carried by labels.

**Token discipline.** 20 raw hexes / 34 uses outside `@theme`, 20+ untokenised white washes, three notice text colours outside the palette, plus hard-coded colours in components (VIS-18).

### 3.4 Visual hierarchy

- **Dashboard:** the eye lands on the 2×2 camera wall (largest, brightest element) — correct. But the wall is only 880 px of 1 640 px content width at 1920, and the third column ("Today by camera") plus the right rail absorb attention with 3 status chips and 5 red priority badges; the top-right primary "Playback" (teal, 36 px) is the loudest *action* while the page's actual task is watching. The stat strip is legible but under-informs: four 401 px cards containing 20 px numbers (VIS-08).
- **Events:** hierarchy **inverts** — 19 red "High" pills plus 20 teal "New" chips form a colour column that out-shouts the event titles and thumbnails. The selected row's teal wash is clear, and the inspector (400 px, sticky) correctly owns the right edge, but two filter systems above it (VIS-22) and three pills per row create "badge soup".
- **Notifications:** the boldest marks are the status pills (Sent green / Suppressed grey), which sit at the extreme right edge of 1 640 px rows — the status is severed from the content it describes (VIS-08).
- **Settings → Object detection:** four separate representations of one state — "Worker stopped" pill, "Stopped" status dot+word, "Disabled" badge, "Detection is off" notice — plus a save bar floating over the table (VIS-07). The screen over-communicates "off" and under-communicates what to do.
- **Login:** the strongest hierarchy in the product: 46 px statement, 44 ch lede, three checkpoints, and one teal CTA. (Its size also breaks the type spec — VIS-15.)
- **Focus mode:** hierarchy is nearly right — full-bleed video with only camera name, Exit, Live, status — but the picture is pillarboxed to 69 % of the screen and the 44 px Unmute button plus Live chip still sit on the picture (`audit/screenshots/states/dashboard-focus-desktop.png`).

### 3.5 Responsive

**1920×1080:** live wall **880×660** with all four tiles above the fold (last caption bottom ≈1 040 of 1 080); content 1 640 px wide; right rail ends early (VIS-08). Dashboard stat cards 4-up.

**1440×900 (laptop):** 10 px horizontal overflow in the dashboard scroll region (VIS-14), 48 px below the fold, stat row 4-up, sidebar 232 px. The frame widths stay 1 640; event rows are still 1 224 px.

**834×1112 (tablet):** sidebar hidden, bottom nav 5×166.8×52 px (good targets), app bar carries brand + status pill + labelled notifications + clock + avatar = **496 px of actions**; camera wall stays 2-up (2×377 px) — the 720 px breakpoint is what collapses to 1-up, so the 834 band is a wide 2-up tablet, which reads well in `audit/screenshots/tablet/dashboard.png`. Two failures in this band: the Object-detection table is **860 px in a 784 px card** (VIS-01) and the sticky save bar **covers two table rows** (VIS-07). The events filter panel correctly collapses to a button here, and the event inspector becomes a bottom sheet (well executed in `audit/screenshots/states/events-mobile-inspector.png`).

**1024 boundary:** sidebar **and** bottom nav both visible at exactly 1024 px (VIS-11) — the one pixel width where the product shows two competing navigations plus the bottom-bar padding.

**390×844 (mobile):** single column; page gutter 12 px (not the specified 14); camera tiles 364×273 with the same four overlays; events filter panel full-height behind the fixed nav; tabs/segments fit one row (5×69 px). Failures: settings tables (VIS-01), sticky bar 112 px + 1 px nav overlap (VIS-07), 15×15 checkboxes (VIS-10), 204 px trailing dead space (VIS-24). The mobile More sheet (`states/mobile-more-sheet.png`) and the events inspector sheet are the best-behaved overlays: 6 targets ≥44 px, handle, backdrop, current-location highlight.

**Long pages:** `/account` 26 817 px desktop / **35 396 px mobile** (VIS-13); `/faces` 1 977 / 6 681 (tablet) / 4 748 (mobile) but paginated; `/notifications` 3 268 / 4 518 paginated at 25/page; `/events` 2 012; `/settings/detection` 4 124 mobile. Long-page behaviour is therefore: lists that paginate stay manageable, the one list that doesn't (sessions) explodes. Fixed overlays do not double-scroll (the shell scrolls `#main` internally at ≥768 px — measured `mainClientHeight 844` at 900 px viewport, i.e. the document itself is not the scroller), which is a good structural choice.

## 5. What works well

1. **Text contrast is excellent and honest.** Every text token pair passes AA with headroom (`ink` 15.82:1, `ink-2` 11.50:1, `muted` 6.34:1, `faint` 5.44:1, accent 11.18:1, accent-ink on accent 10.43:1); all status text on its tint passes (6.07–8.71:1); the worst non-separator rendered pair in 68 captures is 5.20:1. The spec's contrast table is accurate — a rare thing.
2. **Status is never colour-only.** Online/Checking/Sent/Suppressed/Failed/Idle all pair a dot or glyph with a word; the More sheet and inspector use text + icon; the timeline legend names its colours. This is a genuine accessibility win.
3. **Real dark-theme surface system.** Five surface steps, hairlines, soft tinted fills, backdrop blur on app bar/sheets and a restrained shadow for floating layers — the depth reads as a console, not a flat dark page, and there is no light-mode bleed anywhere.
4. **The shell is structurally right.** Grouped nav, sticky sidebar that scrolls internally, breadcrumb + clock + status cluster, one h1 per page (verified `h1Count = 1` in all 68 captures), skip link, 2 px focus ring at 12:1, reduced-motion honoured for pulse/shimmer.
5. **The live wall is the hero.** 4:3 preserved, video wrapper first child, `object-contain`, no WebRTC teardown in focus mode; all four tiles above the fold at 1920 and focus mode is a genuine full-screen operator view.
6. **The login screen is a real piece of design**, on-brand and reassuring without decoration.
7. **Mobile navigation and sheets are well engineered**: 52 px bottom items, correct `aria-current`, backdrop, Escape handling, safe-area padding, and a bottom-sheet inspector that is arguably better than the desktop two-pane on a phone.
8. **Mono/sans discipline is mostly respected** and tabular numerals are used where they matter (clock, stat values, table numerics).

## 7. Score: **6.5 / 10**

**Why not higher**

- A primary admin screen (Settings → Object detection) is **visibly broken at 390 px and 834 px** (VIS-01), and the same defect hits Settings → System at 390 px. A second core screen (Playback) dedicates 59 % of its stage to black bars (VIS-06).
- The product's **primary datum is inconsistent**: 12 h in the clock and filters vs 24 h everywhere else, on the same screen (VIS-02). For a CCTV console, time notation is a correctness issue, not a style one.
- The design system's **own promises are unmet in three measurable places** — minimum type size (9–10 px, VIS-04), 4 px rhythm (58 % off-grid, VIS-16), and the priority/failure colour separation (VIS-03) — and the mobile tap-target contract is missed by 70 % of controls (VIS-10).
- **Hierarchy inverts** on the event timeline: red priority badges out-shout the events, and the wide-screen layout is mostly empty (VIS-08, VIS-22).

**Why not lower**

- The **colour and contrast work is genuinely strong** and measured: all text ≥ AA except decorative separators; status never colour-only; a real 5-step dark surface system.
- The **structural UX of the shell, live wall, inspector sheet, bottom nav and login** is sound and pleasant; one h1 per page; focus/reduced-motion/skip-link handled.
- The **drift is consolidatable**: no redesign is needed to reach 8/10 — fix the two settings tables, unify time notation, map HIGH to amber, raise the 9–10 px floor, snap spacing, and cap the playback stage.

## 8. What I could not verify

1. **12 h vs 24 h in native time inputs is locale-dependent.** The captures (and my session) run Chromium with an en-US locale, which renders `input[type=time]` as "12:00 AM". On an en-IN/24 h locale the same control may render "00:00". The *clock* 12 h issue (VIS-02) is unconditional; the input half needs confirmation on the operator's actual browser.
2. **Empty states and confirmation dialogs** — the fixed dataset produced no empty list and the capture set contains no delete/confirm dialog, so those components were reviewed from source only, not rendered.
3. **Hover/pressed states** — static captures and HTTP-only sessions cannot show hover; `.camera__action` (hover-reveal) is dead code, so its intended look was not observable. Focus rings were verified from CSS (`outline 2px #2FE3C6`, 12.2:1) but not from rendered keyboard-focus pixels.
4. **Text over live video content.** Overlay text contrast was computed over the dark gradient + black frame that the captures show (clock 9.71:1, signal 15.65:1). A bright video frame can reduce this; the overlays do carry text-shadows, but I could not test the worst-case frame.
5. **Playback video metadata** — the player's video had no loaded media in my session (`videoWidth = 0`), so letterboxing was measured from the lead's screenshots (pixel analysis) and from stage geometry, not from live intrinsic size.
6. **Real devices / touch, safe-area insets, 200 % zoom reflow, print, and OS high-contrast** were not tested. The 390 px capture approximates mobile; the 834 band was tested directly.
7. **The playback calendar popover (`react-day-picker` `.rdp-app` overrides)** — not present in the captures or in my session (the Playback date control rendered as a native date field), so its styling could not be judged.
8. **Rendering-engine cross-checks** — everything was measured in Chromium only; Safari/Firefox font metrics (`-apple-system`, `SF Mono` fallbacks) may shift line-length and truncation measurements slightly.
